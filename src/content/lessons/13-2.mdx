<Hook>

A hand can have respectable showdown equity yet repeatedly fold before the river when out of position.

</Hook>

<Idea>

Raw equity assumes the hand reaches showdown against the modeled opponents. Equity realization compares what a strategy actually captures with that benchmark. Position affects information and control: acting last lets you observe a bet or a check before choosing your response. Out of position, a weak draw may face a price that prevents it from using its full showdown equity.

One illustrative model multiplies raw equity by a capture factor. <RealizationInputs /> The notebook turns the reduced average into a binary toy payoff so you can simulate it. The factor is an assumption, not something determined by hole cards alone.

Realization is not always below one: position, folds, and additional value bets can produce more profit than a passive showdown benchmark. A single scalar hides which paths changed. Prefer an explicit action tree when you know those paths; use a factor only to test sensitivity. The simplified range coach cannot infer a guaranteed capture factor from its equity sample.
