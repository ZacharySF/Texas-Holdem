<Idea>

Monte Carlo estimation repeats the same random experiment and averages a measured quantity. Define the target first: an event indicator, a pot share, or a chip payoff. Then sample from the intended distribution with card removal and range weights respected. Increasing samples cannot repair a biased sampling procedure or a wrong event definition.

When an exact answer exists, put it beside the estimate and inspect the signed gap. A fixed seed reproduces the same sequence, which makes comparisons and debugging possible. It does not create additional independent evidence: rerunning that seed repeats the experiment. The workers use a documented seeded generator and rejection sampling for bounded integers.

The standard error of independent sample means decreases in proportion to one divided by the square root of sample count. Multiply the sample count by four to halve that error. The exact ratio is computed below. A rare event can still record no successes; a sensible proportion interval should retain a positive upper bound. For average chip awards, use the variance of awards rather than a binary win formula.
