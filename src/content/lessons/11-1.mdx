<Idea>

Equity is the average share of the pot across legal runouts. An outright win earns the whole pot, a loss earns none, and tied winners divide it equally. Remove both hands first, deal every legal board, evaluate both best five-card hands, and average those shares. Exact enumeration gives a rational answer; a seeded sample estimates the same quantity with an interval.

Pair against two overcards is often called a coin flip. It is only a description of a roughly balanced contest, not an exact equality. The pair begins ahead; either opposing rank may pair, and straights, flushes, or a shared board can change the result. Against just one overcard there are fewer rank-pairing routes. Against undercards, the lower hand must improve more substantially. A bigger pocket pair also blocks many outcomes on which the smaller pair merely remains a pair.

Use the matchup selector to compare the same physical cards under all four structures. “Pair versus overcards” is a family, not one probability: suits and connected ranks matter.
