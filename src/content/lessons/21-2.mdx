<Hook>

An insurer offers to pay you a fixed benefit only if your all-in hand loses outright. What premium makes that extra contract fair?

</Hook>

<Idea>

Define the contract before calculating: which event triggers payment, how much is paid, and when is the premium charged? A tie is not an outright loss. A capped loss reimbursement is different from a fixed benefit. The calculator uses a fixed benefit on a zero pot share, with no payment on ties.

Let L indicate an insured loss: it equals one on that event and zero otherwise. With benefit D and premium pi, the net insurance cash flow is LD minus pi. Multiply each cash flow by its probability and add. Because the premium is paid in either outcome, its expectation is just the premium. The fair premium is loss probability times benefit. A loading is an extra fraction of that fair premium, not a fraction of the entire pot.

The worked example uses a supplied loss chance, benefit 100, and the loading shown in the notebook. The worker generates the two contract outcomes and compares the mean with the exact expected cost. In the linked card example, loss probability comes from enumerating legal rivers for exposed aces against kings. Do not substitute win probability when ties or different payout triggers are possible.

Fair pricing does not mean identical risk. Insurance can offset the same loss that harms your poker result, making their combined cash flow less variable. Positive loading transfers expected wealth to the seller. Running twice instead divides exposure between card outcomes without adding a seller’s premium. Neither arrangement makes a bad call profitable merely by reducing variance.
