<Hook>

A draw needs the river, but calling the flop may lead to another bet on the turn. What does the whole path cost?

</Hook>

<Idea>

An EV tree has decision nodes, where you choose an action, and chance nodes, where outcomes occur with stated probabilities. At a chance node multiply each child value by its branch chance and add. At a decision node compare the child values only after defining each action’s costs and later policy. Work backward from final chip payoffs so earlier costs are counted once.

The worked toy tree starts with two hundred in the pot, a first call of fifty, and a later opposing bet of fifty that you commit to call. A final win returns the existing pot plus the later opposing money; a loss costs both calls. The supplied final win chance prices this specific policy. Pricing only the first call while using river equity overstates its value. These are effective odds: the full expected cost of following the draw, not just today’s price.

A richer tree can split the turn into hit and miss branches and choose a different policy after each. Multiply conditional turn and river chances along each path, then add disjoint final paths. Do not assume an opponent pays the same bet on every river; hitting an obvious draw may stop their betting. The diagram below is deliberately small enough to audit every leaf.
