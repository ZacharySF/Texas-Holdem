<Hook>

One player can have higher average equity while the other retains more of the strongest possible hands.

</Hook>

<Idea>

An equity distribution assigns an equity to each possible hand or combo in a range, then weights those values by the range. Its average is range equity. Range advantage usually refers to an advantage in that average against the opposing range on the same board. Nut advantage concerns the strongest portion: which player can hold more hands near the top of the possible strengths. Define the relevant strong-hand set before counting it.

A polarized betting range mixes very strong hands with weak hands that may benefit from folds. A merged range includes more medium-strength value hands. These are descriptions of shape, not proof that a bet is balanced or optimal. The formal bluff-mix and game-theory calculations belong to a later chapter. Here you can construct both illustrative shapes in the grid and inspect their legal combos.

The heatmap estimates each hand class against random cards or your selected opposing range. Classes with more physical combos deserve greater weight when computing a range average. A cell average can hide suit-specific differences on a suited board; use specific-hand equity to inspect those details. Its interval is sampling uncertainty, not the spread across classes. The histogram and cell list describe that separate spread.
