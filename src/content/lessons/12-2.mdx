<Hook>

The pot was one hundred. An opponent bets fifty, so the pot you can win now is one hundred fifty. You must call fifty.

</Hook>

<Idea>

Let P include the opposing bet and C be your call. If your showdown share is e, the final pot is P plus C and your expected award is e times that amount. Subtract C because those chips leave your stack now. Folding risks no additional chips, so its value from this decision is zero. Solving call EV equal to zero gives the break-even share C divided by P plus C.

Rake removes money from the final pot. With a fixed deduction R, replace the final pot by P plus C minus R before multiplying by equity. The worked experiment uses a fixed rake; actual schedules can have caps, exclusions, and per-hand rules. Its threshold is therefore higher than the no-rake threshold. The Call or Fold drill states its rake explicitly.

Implied odds include additional money you expect to win after improving. Reverse implied odds include additional losses when an apparently improved hand remains beaten. Neither is free money guaranteed by your outs. Specify the later action and conditional payoff before adding it to today’s EV. Next chapter builds that tree. If you must pay again to see the river, two-card equity alone does not price today’s call.

<AtTheTable>

Use the pot including the bet you face, and subtract only new risk. State rake and future-betting assumptions before grading a decision.

</AtTheTable>

<QuantCorner>

Transaction costs reduce a trade’s expected profit just as rake reduces a pot. A small apparent edge can disappear once those costs are included.

</QuantCorner>
