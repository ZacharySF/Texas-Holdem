<Idea>

A range is a distribution over possible hole-card combinations. Start by listing physical combos, remove every combo containing a known card, then normalize their remaining weights. The grid groups combos into pair, suited, and offsuit classes for editing; equal class weights do not make equally probable classes, because class sizes differ.

Weights are relative frequencies, not independent promises that several overlapping combos can occur together. In a multiway deal, different players cannot share a card. The engine samples the product of their supplied priors and rejects joint assignments with collisions. This conditions the whole assignment on legality; sampling each range once and silently repairing collisions would change the distribution.

The computed ace example counts remaining combos after one exposed ace. Combo Counter asks the same physical-card question with seeded blockers. In the range Lab, each grid cell shows its count after board and dead-card removal. The hand-versus-range calculation additionally removes your specified hand. State which known cards are being removed before comparing those counts.
