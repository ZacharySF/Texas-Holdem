<Hook>

You get all the chips in while ahead, then lose on the river. The result is real, but it is not the expected award at the commitment point.

</Hook>

<Idea>

All-in EV adjustment replaces an actual pot award with the average award over legal remaining runouts when further decisions can no longer change that pot. Subtract the same contributions from both versions. This keeps the units and starting point consistent. The expected difference is zero under the runout model, while its realized difference records late card luck.

In multiway play, each side pot has different eligibility and may become fixed at a different time. A short stack can be all in while two deeper stacks continue betting. Stats searches for each pot’s fixed participants and contributions, then averages its awards at that point. A subsequent fold can delay that point. Unadjusted pots keep their actual awards. The method uses revealed cards after the hand; the live coach never receives those hidden cards.

The adjusted line is not a strategy score. It retains luck in hole cards, cooler matchups, earlier boards, and the situations in which you chose to commit. Sampling error in an estimated award is another distinct source of uncertainty. Stats reports its numerical uncertainty separately from the much larger uncertainty about a long-run win rate.
