### Arrange every label

The product of descending positive integers from a number down to one is its **factorial**. An exclamation mark names that product. It is notation for an operation, not an instruction to become excited. For three labels, expand it into three choices, then two choices, then one choice; each choice corresponds to an actual position.

### Stop early for a partial arrangement

Suppose four cards A, B, C, D are available, but you deal only three positions. The first position has four choices, the second three, the third two. Stop there. You are not arranging the final unused card into another recorded position.

For n distinct cards and k ordered positions, take k factors: n, then one less, continuing until the k-th factor. The notation n names how many cards were available; k names how many positions were filled. Those letters stand for numbers, not new kinds of arithmetic.

<Formula tex={'n(n-1)\\cdots(n-k+1)=\\frac{n!}{(n-k)!}'} />

The dots mean continue the descending pattern. In the fraction, the denominator cancels the factors belonging to positions you never filled. You can check it by expanding the two factorials and crossing out matching factors. A **permutation** is such an ordered arrangement; the term does not introduce a second counting mechanism.

### Why zero factorial equals one

After every card has been placed, there is exactly one way to place no remaining cards: do nothing. That empty arrangement gives zero factorial the value one. It also lets the partial-arrangement formula work when k equals n, because no unwanted factor remains in the denominator.

This is not saying zero cards can be physically dealt as a card. It counts completed arrangements, and the empty arrangement is one possible completion of an already finished row.

### Count outcomes at the right level

The full list above has equal probability under a fair shuffle because the labels refer to distinct physical cards. If two cards happen to share a rank, they are still different cards, such as As and Ah. A rank pattern may collect several physical arrangements. Do not treat distinct patterns as equally likely merely because each has a short name.

The simulator uses three labeled cards, not a standard deck with an unspecified three-card target. Each trial shuffles those three labels and checks the complete requested row. There is one favorable row among all factorially many complete rows.

### A useful boundary question

Before calculating, ask: “If I swap two selected cards, is that a new outcome for my question?” For a recorded sequence, yes. For your unordered hole-card hand, no. The next chapter takes the ordered count you now understand and removes those duplicate descriptions deliberately.
